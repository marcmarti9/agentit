# Audio, captions and finishing

Read when an ad includes speech, captions, music or SFX. A silent ad may be
intentional; do not make a generated soundtrack a universal quality gate.

Use an approved script/transcript. Correct names, product terms, numbers and
negation from the actual recording. Preserve speech meaning and qualifiers;
word highlighting is a style choice, not a prerequisite for accessible captions.
Maintain caption start/end times in a declared unit, language, speaker where
needed and line breaks at meaningful boundaries. Caption units may be seconds
or milliseconds while a composition uses frames: convert with the actual FPS,
declare rounding and test the resulting cue boundaries. Avoid silent drift at
non-integer frame rates or after trims/time stretching.

Budget enough display time for actual wording and audience. Review at target
viewing size and playback speed, including long translated terms and final
lines. Keep caption backgrounds/contrast stable on footage, preserve safe areas
and avoid collisions with interface chrome, proof, product detail or the CTA.
Burned captions and sidecar subtitles are different deliverables; choose what
the destination supports and verify current requirements if material.

Music/SFX support emphasis and continuity. Record source/license, trim, fades,
loop seams and voice/beat cue positions. Duck or rebalance music for speech;
check audible intelligibility and measured peaks/loudness where tooling permits.
Do not prescribe one loudness target for every channel. Avoid clipping,
accidental silence, abrupt tails or a soundtrack implying unsupported urgency.

After the final encode, inspect stream presence, start offsets and channel
layout; play speech near cuts and the final tail. A source waveform does not
prove exported sync. Keep the transcript/subtitle file, audio source map and
editable mix decisions with the delivery; label any listening/device checks
that were unavailable.
