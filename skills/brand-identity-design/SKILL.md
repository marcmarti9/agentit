# Brand Identity Design

Translate approved positioning into a usable visual system that survives its actual contexts.
This skill owns identity decisions and cross-media stress tests. It does not invent business
positioning, run a production shoot, or replace an existing design system's component ownership.

## Use when

- A new or refreshed identity needs marks, colour, type, imagery, iconography, or motion rules.
- An identity looks inconsistent across web, packaging, social, product, signage, or documents.
- A team needs a bounded identity specification before applying it to multiple deliverables.

## Do not use when

- Positioning, audience, name, or the core promise is still undecided; resolve those inputs first.
- Briefing a specific photo, film, illustration, or campaign production; use the appropriate art
  direction owner with the approved identity as input.
- Building UI tokens, components, or implementation governance; use `design-system-engineering`.
- Making one logo export or one social post without a system decision.

## Inputs and boundary

Collect approved positioning, audience/category context, legal or parent-brand constraints,
application inventory, languages/scripts, accessibility needs, budget, and current assets. Treat
reference work as inspiration, never a template to imitate. Identify who will update routine
assets after launch and use their existing approved tool or asset library where it fits.

## Procedure

1. Write a one-page identity premise: audience, intended recognition, emotional register, and
   explicit exclusions. Resolve conflicts with binding brand or legal constraints before drawing.
2. Inventory the real applications and select stress cases: small digital mark, monochrome use,
   long/short name, limited-colour reproduction, responsive page, and any required physical use.
3. Build a mark system only to the extent the contexts require: primary signature, compact mark,
   and lockups where justified. Test silhouette, small-size legibility, single-colour reproduction,
   clear space, and misuse; do not manufacture variants to meet a quota.
4. Define colour as roles and relationships: identifiers, surfaces, text, emphasis, and semantic
   states where needed. Record reproducible values for the intended media, contrast results, and
   non-colour signals for important states. A screen value is not a print specification.
5. Define type and image behaviour in cooperation with `typography-and-layout`: families or binding
   faces, role rules, rights status, fallback, crop/composition, subjects, lighting, and exclusions.
   State icon geometry and motion intent only if those assets are in scope.
6. Apply the system to the selected stress cases. Check recognition, contrast, hierarchy, language
   expansion, cropping, small-size behaviour, and whether a third party can use it consistently.
7. Document decisions as reusable rules, approved assets, and examples. Keep mutable logos, images,
   and templates in the client-appropriate existing asset or administration surface, not hidden in
   narrative instructions.
8. Record any approvals still needed. Never automatically activate, publish, replace, or roll out
   the identity from a proposed specification.

## Review questions

| Area | Ask | Failure to catch |
|---|---|---|
| Mark | Does it survive the smallest required context and one colour? | A handsome unusable logo |
| Colour | Can intended text and states be understood without colour alone? | Inaccessible or ambiguous UI |
| Type | Are roles, coverage, and rights workable in each medium? | Substitution or unreadable localisation |
| Imagery | Do composition and subject rules distinguish the brand? | Asset drift into generic stock treatment |
| Motion | Does it reinforce meaning and respect reduced-motion needs? | Decorative or harmful movement |

## Handoff

Package only the approved mark variants and clear usage rules required by the application list.
Record file formats, colour-space intent, licensing/rights status, alt-text or descriptive needs,
and the accountable asset owner. A brand guide can guide production; it does not certify legal
clearance or operational activation.

## Common failures

- Starting with a polished hero mark and discovering it fails in a favicon, embroidery, or a
  long translated label.
- Treating contrast as a palette preference rather than a contextual test.
- Leaving imagery direction as mood words without subjects, compositions, and exclusions.
- Delivering raw working files with no designated routine-update location for the client.

## Completion record

List included and deferred elements, approved variants, tested contexts, unresolved rights or
legal questions, and the next accountable decision. This record prevents a concept presentation
from being mistaken for a release package.

## Evidence and degradation

Use renders, physical samples, or clearly labelled mockups appropriate to the applications.
Without actual assets, a target medium, rights evidence, or a renderer, provide a proposed
identity specification and list what cannot yet be validated. Do not claim originality clearance,
trademark availability, contrast compliance, print match, or final asset readiness without the
relevant review.

## Acceptance

- A premise and application inventory bound the identity decisions.
- Each included element has a purpose, usable rule, and an exclusion or misuse case.
- Stress-case evidence or an explicit pending-evidence list accompanies the specification.
- The deliverable identifies the approved owner and surface for routine future asset updates.
