# Motion Graphics Production

Make a time-based sequence that communicates a specific idea. This skill owns
the authored production plan and review loop; it does not choose a rendering
engine by habit. Pair it with a selected technical adapter only after the
project and delivery constraints make that adapter appropriate.

## Trigger and boundary

Use when the outcome is a video or rendered sequence with a beginning, ordered
beats, and a final media delivery. Typical jobs are an animated logo, a launch
explainer, a product sequence, kinetic typography, a short editorial sequence,
or a video assembled from supplied footage and visual assets.

Do not use for a website animation, scroll experience, component feedback,
motion diagnosis, generic advertising strategy, generated-image prompting, or
an existing editor project that needs its own authorised adapter. A request to
“make it dynamic” is insufficient: establish the medium and intended audience
first.

## Establish the production contract

Collect only information that determines the result:

1. Name the audience, single message, delivery destination, required aspect
   ratios, duration range, frame rate if prescribed, and the decision or
   feeling the sequence should leave behind.
2. Inventory supplied logo/vector/product assets, footage, copy, narration,
   music and sound effects. Record provenance and permissions for material that
   will actually ship. Do not treat a reference clip, website, or generated
   image as permission to reuse its content.
3. Separate confirmed facts and approved claims from placeholders. Keep
   invented copy, estimates, and missing assets visibly labelled so they are
   reviewed before delivery.
4. Choose the smallest production form that explains the message: type and
   shape composition, screen/product capture, supplied footage, vector artwork,
   or a genuine 3D shot. Do not simulate product truth that the available asset
   cannot support.
5. Name the renderer only when the current project, installed tooling, and
   required output justify it. Rendering is an ordinary local task when the
   user authorised it. Assess acquisition, installation, provider connection,
   billed generation and publication against the actual task/host authority;
   skill selection alone grants none of them. Preserve existing authorization.

## Write the beat sheet before building

For every scene, record its purpose, source asset, text or narration, entry,
resting pose, exit/hand-off, local duration, and what a viewer should understand
by its end. A beat may be a still hold when reading or product inspection needs
time. Let continuity carry a position, shape, camera direction, colour or idea
only when it explains a relationship; a cut is often clearer.

Make at least these decisions explicit:

- opening state and hook;
- progression of evidence or product understanding;
- climax/payoff and calm ending/CTA where required;
- key poses at meaningful starts, settles, cuts, and final frame;
- type hierarchy, reading order, safe margins, and caption treatment;
- audio/narration/SFX intent, rights status, and silent fallback;
- variant strategy for portrait, landscape, square, or reduced asset sets.

Read [production-verification.md](references/production-verification.md) when
writing the beat sheet, working with captions/audio, adapting an aspect ratio,
or preparing render evidence.

## Build an authored sequence

Use a shared visual system for approved type, colour, spacing and graphic
language, while allowing individual scenes to differ when the story needs
contrast. Keep timing named and editable rather than spreading anonymous frame
numbers across scenes. Construct each scene in local time so moving a scene in
the master order does not require reauthoring its internal animation.

For type, animate only what improves hierarchy, word order, emphasis, or
rhythm. A logo reveal should respect the supplied mark's geometry and clear
space; do not fragment it merely to create activity. Product shots must retain
recognisable proportions, material cues, labels and factual claims. Use texture,
camera motion, grain, glow, parallax, blur, animation density, and sound only
when each has a scene-specific reason. No universal stack, transition style, or
motion curve establishes quality.

Plan captions from the approved transcript or timed text. Keep them readable
against the actual image, avoid placing essential words under platform chrome,
and preserve meaning when captions, sound, or motion are unavailable. Audio
beats can inform cuts, but must not force unlicensed music or obscure narration.

## Variants and optional branches

Reflow from semantic layout anchors and scene intent, not by letterboxing or
blindly scaling a composition. Re-check type wrapping, safe areas, focal crops,
and hand-offs in every requested ratio.

Use a format/runtime branch only when it is required:

- **Lottie:** when a defined vector-animation export/import contract is part of
  the deliverable; verify the target's supported feature set.
- **Rive:** when a supplied `.riv` asset and interactive state-machine behavior
  are required by an approved runtime; it is not a default video renderer.
- **Existing Adobe After Effects project:** only through an authorised project
  workflow with its own read/write and rollback discipline.
- **3D:** only when a usable model and authored camera/material story add
  information that 2D cannot communicate adequately.

## Review and delivery

Run the project’s type/build checks, then inspect a low-cost preview across the
whole sequence. Inspect stills at each key pose and at every potentially rough
cut, dense type moment, caption, logo, product label, and final resting frame.
Render the requested final media only after these checks resolve the observed
issues. Decode or extract representative frames from the *final encoded file*;
a preview alone does not prove the delivery encode.

Report the delivered composition/version, aspect ratios, duration/fps, source
assets and rights assumptions, checked moments, altered/invented copy, and
known limits. Do not claim audio sync, caption accuracy, platform-safe area,
colour accuracy, licensing, or delivery publication unless it was actually
verified for that output.

## Completion criteria

The work is complete when a viewer can follow the intended message; the beat
sheet maps to the rendered sequence; required facts and assets are traceable;
each requested variant has a deliberate layout; normal and silent/caption
requirements are addressed where in scope; and preview plus final-encode
evidence names what was inspected and what remains unverified.
